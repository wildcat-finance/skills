# Rewriting

What to do once the lint finds something. Absorbed from upstream (see `NOTICE.md`), compressed.

## Order of operations

1. Lock the facts. Named entities, numbers, dates, URLs, citations, quotations, technical claims, explicit uncertainty, and the author's stance do not move.
2. Separate the brief from the artefact. Long inputs mix material with instructions about the material. Instructions address the editor, not the reader, and must not appear in the output. Reprinting a brief is the same class of error as inventing content.
3. Cut filler and inflated abstract nouns.
4. Make each sentence carry something: a claim, an example, a constraint, an image, a number, a consequence, or an argumentative move. A sentence that carries none is filler regardless of how clean it reads.
5. Match the author's register.
6. Check for meaning loss.

## Evidence-bound mode

When the source gives only vague copy, do not invent product features, dates, people, metrics, workflows, examples, or outcomes. Unsupported claims such as "faster decisions", "better alignment", or "reduced friction" become proof gaps, questions, or explicitly attributed claims.

Do not launder a vague outcome into a polished one. If the source gives only an abstract benefit, name the missing mechanism, owner, metric, or evidence instead of restating the benefit as true.

## Preservation

Qualifiers that carry scope, uncertainty, causality, risk, or legal meaning survive every rewrite. "Broadly" in a market summary is filler. "Broadly" in a covenant is a defined scope.

In support copy, do not add process promises unless the source says the action is available. In policy and incident copy, do not add quality labels such as "auditable", "secure", or "resilient" unless the source states that property directly.

## Ways the rewrite itself fails

Replacing one formula with another. Clipped aphorisms, tidy triads, dramatic fragments, and generic consultant voice are each their own slop.

Over-editing text that was already good. A light edit, or leaving a passage alone, is a valid output.

Bland-clean prose: every marker removed and nothing said. Check density after cutting, not just marker count.

Cadence collapse: uniform sentence length and repeated openers survive vocabulary edits and read as machine-made on their own.
